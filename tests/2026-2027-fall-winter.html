Question 1 4 points

Rain falls vertically at $8.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ relative to the ground. A pedestrian walks horizontally at $3.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$. To point the umbrella's central shaft into the apparent direction of the incoming rain, through what angle should she tilt it forward from the vertical?

  • A. $17.0^\circ$
  • B. $20.6^\circ$
  • C. $22.0^\circ$
  • D. $69.4^\circ$
  • E. $0^\circ$ (vertical)

Question 2 4 points

Two masses, $3.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ and $2.0\,\mathrm{kg}$, hang from opposite ends of a taut, light, inextensible string passing over a frictionless, massless pulley. What is the magnitude of their acceleration? Use $g=9.80\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$.

  • A. $0.98\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
  • B. $3.92\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
  • C. $4.90\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
  • D. $1.96\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$
  • E. $9.80\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$

  • B. $6.12\,\mathrm{cm}$
  • C. $4.12\,\mathrm{cm}$

Question 4 4 points

  • C. $4.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$

Question 5 4 points

A copper wire has length $60.96\,\mathrm{m}$, radius $0.814\,\mathrm{mm}$, and resistance $0.530\,\Omega$ at room temperature. A current of $10.0\,\mathrm{A}$ is switched on. What is the initial rate of increase of the wire's temperature?

Use a copper density of $8960\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$ and a specific heat capacity of $385\,\mathrm{J/(kg\cdot K)}$. Neglect heat loss and assume the wire's temperature is uniform.

  • A. $0.0625\,\mathrm{K/s}$
  • B. $0.121\,\mathrm{K/s}$
  • C. $0.250\,\mathrm{K/s}$
  • D. $0.500\,\mathrm{K/s}$

  • B. $84.4\,\mathrm{min}$
  • C. $14.7\,\mathrm{min}$
  • D. $24.0\,\mathrm{h}$

Question 7 4 points

A coin lies at the bottom of a tank containing a $20.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ layer of water with refractive index $1.33$, beneath a $20.0\,\mathrm{cm}$ layer of oil with refractive index $1.50$. An observer in air looks nearly vertically down at the coin. How far below the top surface of the oil does the coin appear to be?

Use the small-angle approximation and take the refractive index of air to be $1.00$.

  • A. $40.0\,\mathrm{cm}$
  • B. $30.1\,\mathrm{cm}$
  • C. $26.7\,\mathrm{cm}$
  • D. $56.6\,\mathrm{cm}$
  • E. $28.4\,\mathrm{cm}$

Question 8 4 points

A conducting rod of length $0.40\,\mathrm{m}$ bridges two parallel horizontal rails, remaining perpendicular to them. The rails are connected at one end by a $2.0\,\Omega$ resistor, forming a closed circuit with the rod. A uniform magnetic field of $0.50\,\mathrm{T}$ is perpendicular to the plane of the circuit.

What is the magnitude of the horizontal force needed to keep the rod moving along the rails at a constant $2.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$? Neglect friction, self-inductance, and the resistance of the rod and rails.

  • A. $0.020\,\mathrm{N}$
  • B. $0.040\,\mathrm{N}$
  • C. $0.080\,\mathrm{N}$
  • D. $0.20\,\mathrm{N}$
  • E. $0.40\,\mathrm{N}$

Question 9 4 points

A cannon at ground level launches a ball at speed $v_0$ and angle $\theta$ above a flat, horizontal surface, with $0<\theta<90^\circ$. At each impact, the ball retains a fraction $k$ of the kinetic energy associated with its vertical motion, where $0<k<1$. The surface is frictionless, so the horizontal velocity is unchanged. Neglect air resistance, the ball's size, and the duration of each impact. Let $g$ be the gravitational acceleration.

What is the total time the ball spends in the air, summed over its initial flight and all subsequent bounces?

  • A. $\displaystyle\frac{2v_0\sin\theta}{g(1-k)}$
  • B. $\displaystyle\frac{v_0\sin\theta}{g(1-\sqrt{k})}$
  • C. $\displaystyle\frac{2v_0\sin\theta}{g(1-\sqrt{k})}$
  • D. $\displaystyle\frac{2v_0\cos\theta}{g(1-\sqrt{k})}$
  • E. $\displaystyle\frac{v_0\sin\theta}{2g(1-k)}$

Question 10 4 points

A smooth hemispherical bowl of radius $R$ rotates about its vertical symmetry axis at angular speed $\omega$. A bead of mass $m$ is given an initial velocity such that it moves with the same angular speed as the bowl in a horizontal circle at a fixed height. There is no friction between the bead and the bowl.

Let $\theta$ be the angle between the downward vertical and the line from the sphere's center to the bead, with $0<\theta<90^\circ$. What angular speed $\omega$ allows this motion?

  • A. $\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{g}{R\sin\theta}}$
  • B. $\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{g}{2R\cos\theta}}$
  • C. $\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{2g}{R\cos\theta}}$
  • D. $\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{g\cos\theta}{R}}$
  • E. $\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{g}{R\cos\theta}}$

Question 11 4 points

Replace the hemispherical bowl in Question 10 with a smooth bowl formed by rotating the curve $$z=\sqrt{a r}$$ about the vertical $z$-axis. Here $r$ is the horizontal distance from the axis, $z$ is the height above the bottom, and $a>0$ is a constant with units of length.

The bead moves in a horizontal circle at height $H>0$, below the rim, with the same angular speed $\omega$ as the bowl. What value of $\omega$ allows it to remain at this height?

  • A. $\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{ga}{2H^2}}$
  • B. $\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{ga^2}{2H^3}}$
  • C. $\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{2ga^2}{H^3}}$
  • D. $\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{g}{H}}$
  • E. $\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{gH}{2a^2}}$

Question 12 4 points

A bead is moving in a horizontal circle at a fixed height inside a smooth, rotationally symmetric bowl, as in Questions 10 and 11. The bowl initially rotates at the same angular speed as the bead. At the bead's position, the surface is inclined at an angle $\alpha$ to the horizontal. Let $N_1$ be the normal force on the bead.

The bowl is suddenly stopped by a brake that acts only on the bowl. Its shape and vertical symmetry axis remain unchanged, and there is still no friction. Let $N_2$ be the normal force on the bead immediately after the bowl stops. What is $N_2/N_1$?

  • A. $\sin^2\alpha$
  • B. $\cos\alpha$
  • C. $\cos^2\alpha$
  • D. $\tan^2\alpha$
  • E. $1$

Question 14 6 points

An air-core solenoid has $125$ turns uniformly distributed over a length of $50.0\,\mathrm{mm}$. Each turn has diameter $150\,\mathrm{mm}$, and the current is $30.0\,\mathrm{A}$. What is the magnetic-field magnitude at the midpoint of its central axis?

Model the winding as a thin cylindrical current sheet and neglect the connecting leads. Use $\mu_0=4\pi\times10^{-7}\,\mathrm{T\cdot m/A}$. Do not use the infinite-solenoid approximation.

  • A. $29.8\,\mathrm{mT}$
  • B. $22.6\,\mathrm{mT}$
  • C. $94.2\,\mathrm{mT}$
  • D. $87.4\,\mathrm{mT}$

Question 15 6 points

A plasma is driven by electromagnetic waves of frequency $2.45\,\mathrm{GHz}$. What uniform magnetic-field magnitude is required for fifth-harmonic electron cyclotron resonance? Here fifth harmonic means that the driving frequency is five times the electron cyclotron frequency. Treat the electrons as nonrelativistic.

Use $m_e=9.109\times10^{-31}\,\mathrm{kg}$ and the electron charge magnitude $e=1.602\times10^{-19}\,\mathrm C$.

  • A. $5.41\times10^2\,\mathrm T$
  • B. $2.71\times10^3\,\mathrm T$
  • C. $8.75\times10^{-2}\,\mathrm T$
  • D. $1.75\times10^{-2}\,\mathrm T$
